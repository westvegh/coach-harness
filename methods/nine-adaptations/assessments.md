# Assessments

Field tests for each adaptation. They need little equipment: a tape measure, a hand dynamometer, a leg extension machine, a stopwatch, and a bike, rower or track. The principles behind the order and frequency are in `principles/08-assessment.md` [NA-ASM-001] [NA-ASM-002].

Standards are rough guides, not pass marks. Compare each athlete first with their own previous results. Many qualities decline by roughly 10% a decade after about age 40, so adjust targets for age. Timestamps refer to the episode keys in `sources.md`.

## Test week

Run the battery over about a week, with at least a day between test days. Test fresh qualities first and fatiguing ones last.

| Day | Tests | Why this order |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Body composition, movement quality | No fatigue cost, but recent training distorts them. No hard training in the 48 hours before. |
| 2 | Power, then strength, then muscular endurance | Power and strength need a fully fresh nervous system. Muscular endurance can follow after 15-20 minutes of rest. |
| 3 | Anaerobic capacity, then on a separate day or after full recovery: maximal aerobic output and long duration | These create deep fatigue. |

**Frequency:** the full battery at least once a year, twice is better. Retest a weak area every 1-3 months, under the same conditions (time of day, warm-up, equipment).

## Tests by adaptation

### 1. Skill: movement quality (E1, 01:07:18)

Film a few slow reps of the push-up, pull-up, row, squat and deadlift, from the front and from the side. Check each joint, from the shoulder to the ankle, for symmetry, stability, control and range. Score each lift 0, 1 or 3 [NA-ASM-003]. A 0 means the lift is not loaded until it improves.

### 2. Speed (E1, 01:12:38)

Optional for most adults; useful for field and court athletes. A short timed sprint (for example 10-20 m) or a change-of-direction drill, best of 2-3.

### 3. Power: jumps (E1, 01:12:38)

- **Broad jump:** two-foot take-off, best of 2-3. A good adult standard is a jump about equal to body height for men, and about 15% less for women.
- **Vertical jump:** standing reach subtracted from jump reach, best of 2-3. Around 24 in is a good standard for men (around 20 in past 50), and roughly 15-20% less for women.

### 4. Strength (E1, 01:18:42)

- **Grip (dynamometer):** best of 3 per hand. A rough floor is 40 kg for men and 35 kg for women, and 60 kg or more is a strong result. The two hands should be within about 10% of each other.
- **Leg strength:** a one-rep leg extension of about body weight. Lower the target by about 10% a decade past 40.
- **Squat hold:** hold a goblet or front-rack load of half body weight at the bottom of a squat for 45 seconds. It tests position and leg endurance as well as strength.

### 5. Hypertrophy: muscle mass (E1, 01:27:16)

Fat-free mass index (FFMI) from height, weight and body-fat estimate. About 20 or more for men and 18 or more for women is a good target. Around 17 (men) or 15 (women) and below suggests too little muscle. FFMI is less reliable at high body fat.

### 6. Muscular endurance (E1, 01:29:38)

- **Push-ups:** as many strict reps in a row as possible: full lockout at the top, chest to the floor, no pauses. Any break ends the test. For men, 25 or more is the anchor, 10 to 25 means work to do, and under 10 is a red flag. For women, scale the target down; the source gives no fixed number.
- **Planks:** front plank 60 seconds; side plank 45 seconds per side.

### 7. Anaerobic capacity (E1, 01:36:23)

Thirty seconds all-out on an air bike, rower or sprint track, with heart rate recorded. Then watch recovery: a drop of about 30 beats in the first minute after the effort is a good sign. A slow drop points to a weak aerobic base.

### 8. Maximal aerobic output (E1, 01:39:29)

Estimate VO2max with a field test and an online calculator:

- **12-minute run:** cover as much distance as possible in 12 minutes.
- **1-mile walk:** walk a mile as fast as possible, and record the time and the heart rate at the finish. Use this for untrained or older athletes.

Rough anchors: a VO2max near 18 ml/kg/min is the level where living alone becomes hard. About 35 (men) and 30 (women) are minimum targets, and higher is better (E1, 01:52:44). A resting heart rate under 60 is a good sign; around 75 or more points to low fitness or another cause worth checking.

### 9. Long-duration endurance (E1, 01:42:42)

Twenty minutes or more of continuous running, cycling or rowing, faster than a walk, with no stops, breathing through the nose only. If nasal breathing cannot be held for the whole time, the aerobic base is the limiter.

## After the tests

Record each result with the date and conditions. If the athlete has no clear goal, the weakest adaptation becomes the next block's priority [NA-ASM-004].
