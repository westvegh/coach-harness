# 8. Assessment

Testing turns "I think my endurance is fine" into a number. It shows which adaptation is weakest, and that should shape the next block.

## Order

Test fresh qualities before fatiguing ones [NA-ASM-001]:

1. **Body composition and movement quality first.** They cost no energy, but recent hard training distorts them. Do no hard training for about 48 hours before a body composition measurement.
2. **Skill, power and maximal strength next**, at the start of a session, after a standard warm-up.
3. **Muscular endurance** can follow strength in the same session, after a rest of 15-20 minutes.
4. **Anaerobic capacity, maximal aerobic output and long-duration tests last**, on a separate day. They create deep fatigue.

## Frequency

Run the full battery at least once a year; twice a year is better. Spread it over about a week, for example three test days. Retest a weak area more often, even monthly, and a strong one rarely [NA-ASM-002]. Retest under the same conditions each time: time of day, warm-up, equipment and protocol. Compare results with the athlete's own previous results first, and with norms second.

## Movement quality

Film each main lift (push-up, pull-up, row, squat, deadlift) for a few slow reps, from the front and from the side. Check each joint from the shoulder down to the ankle for symmetry, stability, control and range. Score each lift 0, 1 or 3 [NA-ASM-003]:

- **0:** unsafe to load for now. Fix the movement before adding weight.
- **1:** a minor flaw. Train it, and work on the flaw in the warm-up.
- **3:** good. Train normally.

There is deliberately no score of 2: the scale forces a clear decision.

## Using the results

If the athlete has a clear goal, the tests show what limits it. If not, take the lowest result, relative to the standards, as the priority for the next block of about three months [NA-ASM-004].

The tests, standards and order are listed in `../assessments.md`.
